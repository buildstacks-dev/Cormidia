Cormidia, thinking out loud

So Cormidia is not an app. You install a runtime and point it at an org and at repos. It is supposed to be a standing AI company that runs a portfolio of products while one human sets direction and only shows up for the stuff that can actually hurt. Goals in, verified software outcomes out. Private GitHub is the system of record. The timer wakes the dispatcher, the dispatcher turns due work into a durable episode plan before anyone spends a model turn, then Builder / Reviewer / whoever does the work through a harness adapter, and every tool action hits a gate. Routine work goes. Critical work stops and waits for a real human decision. That is the loop.

What it must do. It must keep valid progress when something crashes mid-claim. It must plan before it routes, unless the human already handed it execution-ready scope with provenance, and "this ticket looks simple" never counts as that. It must treat a harness/model/effort assignment as atomic, not mix and match mid-turn. It must keep role authority separate from which model is running that role. It must fail closed when it cannot classify an effect. It must record approval as "you may do this exact thing" and separately record whether that thing actually executed. It must learn from evidence without smearing one app's secrets or development grants into another app's memory. It must get cheaper and more reliable on comparable work over time, or the whole economic story is fake.

What it must not do. It must not become the human's babysitting job. Cheap-wrong results with hidden retries are not success. It must not prompt-and-pray its way around protocol. It must not contain app code. It must not silently rewrite TASTE, roles, PURPOSE, or pipelines. It must not collapse Builder and Reviewer onto the same provider just because that is convenient. It must not publish, deploy, mutate secrets, touch cloud or DNS, or erase irreversible data without an attributable human approval for that exact action. It must not call a ceiling-stopped or incomplete campaign a pass. It must not read the archived old test corpus for design cues. Grok stays sandbox-only until the vendor risk review is closed. Development grants and raw eval state never leak into the org home.

The cartels, meaning the standing specialist crews under the org roof. Planner / PM synthesizes input and writes tickets. Builder writes the code. Reviewer is deliberately a different agent, different blind spots on purpose. SRE owns infra, CI, deploys, operational fires. Support watches forums and feedback and feeds themes back. Marketing does positioning and changelogs and watches adoption. Distiller turns captured evidence into governed candidates on a daily deterministic precheck. Learning Reviewer does the weekly cross-provider look at those candidates. The human is not a role in the queue. The human is the final authority on critical ops. Below that you also have the mechanical cartels: the dispatcher, the episode planner, the approval queue, the budget and lock machinery, the adapters (claude, codex, pi, and the rest under their own rules). Those are not personalities. They are the company spine. If the spine lies, the personalities do not matter.

How I would want to test this if the bar is ideal production-grade, not "demo worked once."

Start cheap. Unit and hermetic composition tests for every invariant that can be falsified without spending tokens: claim recovery, approval identity, gate classification fail-closed, import direction, no hardcoded default branch, per-claim base freshness, budget ceilings, episode plan validation, grant single-use, approval-versus-execution split. Negative controls first. A detector that has never fired is an assumption.

Then live only where a fake cannot prove the seam: a changed adapter against a real harness, sandbox GitHub smoke, launchd tick with exact loaded identity and clean removal, and a release campaign that refuses to call itself complete if any of those pieces are missing. Human-authored absolute config, pinned commit, spend envelope that is a hard ceiling. Exhaustion means incomplete, never green.

Eval is measurement, not enforcement. Golden sets committed before you tune prompts. Thresholds that are still PROPOSED stay inconclusive. Do not launder data collection into a release pass.

Production-grade also means the operator story is true under interruption. Kill the process mid-claim and show the saga repairs or rearm path. Pause on approval, change nothing material, resume the same pass. Change material context and show it fails closed. Run two apps and prove learning and memory do not cross the boundary. Prove the human is not in the retry loop. Prove the company surfaces a genuine decision or an evidence-backed stop with a next step, not a vibes-based "still working."

Release only on exact candidate evidence: offline suite complete, required live and eval packets current and dispositioned, no missing or stale attestation, no forged approval. If I cannot point at the packet that proves a claim, the claim is not true. That is the whole product, really. A standing company that tells the truth about what it did, what it is waiting on, and what it will not do without you.
